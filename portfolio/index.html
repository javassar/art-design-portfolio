The Sidewalk Waltz: Choreographing Everyday Collision

The complete three-plate architectural and algorithmic study interrogating how physical infrastructure prescribes human behavior. Analyzing an empirical scenario at Vassar College—a rushed pedestrian navigating a narrow sidewalk blocked by a slow-moving group—the project synthesizes real-world aerial site analysis, quadratic modeling, differential vector slope fields, definite calculus integration (73 ft² in-between buffer), photographic stride collage, and an original musical score and choreographic footstep duet ("Sidewalk Waltz"). A multi-disciplinary bridge between calm tech, low-tech craft, and quantitative operations modeling.

Material Craft, Space & Technology

An approach bridging physical prototyping, calm technology, and quantitative problem solving.

1. Restoring Weight, Grain, and Tangible Presence

Much of contemporary technology draws attention into flat, frictionless glass screens. In contrast, calm technology and mindful hardware prioritize human presence, tangible feedback, and the physical world—the rhythm of a walk, the feel of natural materials, and the deliberate click of a physical button. My artistic practice is rooted in material honesty: tearing corrugated cardboard, pulling red embroidery thread through woven canvas, and feeling the drag of charcoal across heavy paper. I bring this tactile sensitivity to product storytelling, spatial environments, and creative technology.

2. Pedestrian Choreography & Behavioral Modeling

In The Sidewalk Waltz, I modeled an everyday encounter—a fast-walking student navigating around a crowd on a campus sidewalk—using calculus integration, differential vector slope fields, photographic stride collage, and an original choreographic score. This work demonstrates how our physical surroundings govern pacing, intimacy, and interaction. When we step away from screen-based distraction, we re-enter this physical choreography.

This practice bridges two complementary capabilities: creating hands-on, low-tech artwork that celebrates presence in the physical world, while applying the quantitative rigor needed for data analysis and operations—such as identifying bottlenecks, analyzing friction points, and optimizing flow under real-world constraints.

3. Habit, Time, and Intentional Living

Across my fine arts work—from the slow hand-embroidery of Jo March’s Maturation to the waking ritual in The Skeleton of Habit—I examine how daily tools and routines shape attention. Rather than treating art as merely decorative, I use it to explore what our tools ask of our focus and how intentional design can help us live more grounded lives.

4. Cross-Disciplinary Fluency

My work unites architectural drafting, physical model-making (scale sketch models and corrugated topography), mathematical modeling, printmaking, and fine arts. I speak the language of engineers and analysts while maintaining the intuitive, hands-on discernment of an artist.
