# Curatorial Strategy & Interview Guide for Jacqueline Adelmann

## 1. Portfolio Positioning & Versatility

This portfolio is positioned across roles that unite physical craft, spatial design, and analytical problem-solving:
1. **Creative Technologist & Artist-in-Residence (Low-Tech / Physical Art):** Creating tangible, analog, and spatial work that celebrates offline presence, material texture, and intentional design.
2. **Data Analyst & Operations Modeling:** Diagnosing real-world bottlenecks, modeling flow dynamics, analyzing friction points, and applying structured quantitative problem solving.
3. **Spatial & Hardware Design:** Understanding physical ergonomics, natural light, scale prototyping, and human-scale movement.

While subtly tailored for companies focused on calm technology, low-tech hardware, and reducing screen fatigue (such as Dumb.co, The Light Phone, Daylight, and Teenage Engineering), the portfolio is intentionally versatile for any team that values creative thinking backed by analytical rigor.

### Key Strengths:
- **Hands-On Material Craft:** Working directly with physical media—cut cardboard, hand embroidery on canvas, darkroom-style photo contact strips, and acoustic musical scoring.
- **Quantitative & Spatial Rigor:** Calculus integration, differential vector slope fields, architectural drafting, scale prototyping, and spatial flow optimization.
- **Human-Scale Inquiry:** Examining how daily tools and physical spaces shape human habits, attention, and interaction.

## 2. The 10 Curated Pieces & How to Speak About Them

### Pillar 1: Spatial, Behavioral & Operational Modeling
1. **The Sidewalk Waltz: Choreographing Everyday Collision (The Complete 3-Plate Study)**
   - *Materials:* Complete 3-plate architectural study (2880×1620): Vassar campus aerial walk-path survey, analog contact stride collage, original musical score notation (*Sidewalk Waltz*), vector slope field differential equations, and definite calculus integral ($73\text{ ft}^2$).
   - *Context:* At Vassar College, a fast-walking student encounters a slow-moving group blocking a narrow walkway toward the Deece (Gordon Commons dining hall). The study analyzes how the pedestrian navigates the architectural constraint to preserve momentum.
   - *For Low-Tech Art / Artist-in-Residence:*
     Focus on the tangible craft of observing offline reality. Rather than using computer simulations, the project uses darkroom-style photographic contact strips, cut paper, hand-drawn vector diagrams, and an original musical score to turn ordinary pedestrian friction into an embodied dance duet.
   - *For Data Analyst & Operations:*
     Focus on the quantitative bottleneck modeling. Explain how you parameterized evasive pedestrian movement using quadratic curve fitting ($f(t) = -\frac{5}{121}(t-11)^2 + 5$), modeled directional velocity with differential vector slope fields, and solved a definite calculus integral to quantify the $73\text{ ft}^2$ spatial capacity required to resolve the bottleneck without stalling flow.
2. **Modeling Negative Space: Physical Ergonomics & The Void**
   - *Materials:* Laminated industrial corrugated cardboard, hand-carved negative space.
   - *Talking Point:* Physical ergonomic study showing a prototype docking over a table edge and conforming to a seated human knee. Highlights hands-on prototyping and understanding physical interfaces.
3. **Curley’s Time Freeze: Kinetic Motion on Raw Strata**
   - *Materials:* Charcoal, ink wash, hot glue, acrylic paint, yarn on corrugated cardboard substrate ($48\times55$).
   - *Talking Point:* Captures human kinetic energy using raw industrial cardboard and fiber, sharing the tactile cardboard language of architectural prototyping.

### Pillar 2: Materiality, Light & Topography
4. **Dual Pavilion: Light, Strata & Dichotomous Nature**
   - *Materials:* Corrugated cardboard topography ($1/16”$), wood dowel structural pavilion ($1/8”$), site sections.
   - *Talking Point:* A contemplative pavilion designed for Larry Bell's light-reactive glass sculptures. Highlights architectural modeling and understanding ambient natural daylight—relevant for reflective, non-emissive display design.
5. **Absence: Architectural Geometry & Minimal Scenography**
   - *Materials:* Hand-cut paper stencils, tape, layered ultramarine spray paint.
   - *Talking Point:* Clean geometric graphic design exploring arches, silent staircases, and luminous orbs. Demonstrates spatial composition and editorial graphic sensibility.

### Pillar 3: Slow Craft, Habit & Presence
6. **Jo March’s Maturation: The Cost of Acceleration**
   - *Materials:* Hand-embroidered red thread on canvas, framed and mounted on reclaimed wood.
   - *Talking Point:* A 5-stage polyptych where technical skill improves with age, but time for slow craft diminishes under accumulating responsibilities. Explores the value of slow, deliberate making in a hurried world.
7. **The Skeleton of Habit: Quantified Mornings**
   - *Materials:* Flowing oil pastel on heavy paper ($18\times24$).
   - *Talking Point:* Explores waking rituals and our daily relationship with alarm clocks. Investigates how daily tools anchor human habit and morning routines.
8. **The Mediated Observer: Optics & Self-Perception**
   - *Materials:* Pen and ink cross-hatching on paper ($14\times17$).
   - *Talking Point:* Captures reflection through refrigerator glass while holding a mechanical camera. Explores how optical tools mediate identity and perception.
9. **Choice Paralysis & The Fig Tree: Modern Overload**
   - *Materials:* Colored pencil on paper.
   - *Talking Point:* Sylvia Plath’s fig tree parable transposed into modern refrigeration bottles. Explores decision fatigue and information overload.
10. **Sensory Surface: Immersion & The Membrane**
    - *Materials:* Oil on canvas ($18\times24$).
    - *Talking Point:* Liquid surface tension and sensory grounding, contrasting physical immersion with digital detachment.

## 3. Conversational Pitch Frameworks

### For Creative Technology & Low-Tech Art:
> "I work at the intersection of spatial design, physical prototyping, and intentional craft. My practice is grounded in hands-on materials—cut cardboard, hand embroidery on canvas, photo contact collages, and acoustic musical scoring. In projects like *The Sidewalk Waltz*, I explore what happens when people look up from their screens and re-engage with physical space: the rhythm of walking, spatial friction, and human connection. I bring this tactile sensibility to product storytelling, spatial design, and creative culture."

### For Data Analyst & Operations Roles:
> "I bring a dual background in spatial design and quantitative problem solving. In *The Sidewalk Waltz*, I approached real-world pedestrian bottlenecks by constructing differential vector slope fields, parameterizing non-linear evasive trajectories, and solving definite calculus integrals to compute spatial buffer capacity. Whether optimizing a user flow, diagnosing friction points in an operational pipeline, or modeling capacity constraints, I approach complex problems with structured analytical clarity and creative execution."
